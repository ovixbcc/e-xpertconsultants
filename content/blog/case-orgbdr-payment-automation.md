Every birth and death in Bangladesh has to be registered, and for most citizens that starts at the **Union Parishad**. Behind this everyday service sits a large payment problem.

## The scale of the challenge

- More than **80,000 registration tickets** are processed every day.
- Fees range from **BDT 50 to 150** per ticket, depending on the service.
- That adds up to an estimated **BDT 24 crore a month**, collected largely **in cash**.
- Around **7,500 Union Parishad booths** reportedly do not pass this government revenue on to the Ministry of Finance as they should.

When money moves in cash across thousands of counters, it is very hard to know how much was collected, where it went and whether it reached the treasury.

## Why cash is the root problem

Cash collection creates gaps at every step: no automatic link between a registration and its fee, manual registers that are easy to alter, delays in depositing money, and no live view for the ministry. Citizens also lose out. They may be asked to pay more than the official fee and rarely get a proper receipt.

## What a digital fee system looks like

**1. Every application gets a payment reference.** When an application is created in the registration system, it generates a unique payment ID with the exact official fee. No fee, no ID; no ID, no certificate.

**2. Citizens pay digitally or at the counter with a digital record.** Payment through mobile financial services such as bKash, Nagad or Rocket, cards, or an assisted counter that records the payment against the reference.

**3. Money settles straight to the government account.** Funds go directly from the payment gateway to the designated treasury account, not through the booth. Booth operators can receive their service charge automatically as a separate split.

**4. Automatic receipts.** Citizens get an SMS receipt showing the official fee they paid, which discourages overcharging.

**5. Live dashboards.** The ministry, divisions and districts can see collections by union in real time, and reconciliation with the treasury becomes automatic instead of a monthly paper exercise.

## Making it work on the ground

Technology is only part of the answer. A rollout of this size needs:

- **A phased launch**, starting with unions that already have good connectivity
- **Incentives for booth operators**, so digital collection is in their interest
- **Assisted payments** for citizens without mobile wallets
- **Training and a help line** for union staff
- **Clear rules** that link certificate issue to a confirmed payment

## Why it matters

Public services run on trust. Digital fee collection protects government revenue, removes the chance to overcharge citizens and frees staff from handling cash. The same model can apply to many other high-volume, low-value government services.
