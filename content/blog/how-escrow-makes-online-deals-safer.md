Every online deal between people who do not know each other starts with the same question: who takes the risk first? If the buyer pays in advance, they worry the product will never arrive or will not match the description. If the seller delivers first, they worry they will never be paid.

In Bangladesh this problem shows up everywhere: Facebook and Instagram shops, freelance projects, wholesale orders, used cars and electronics, and services booked online. Cash on delivery solves part of it, but it does not work for services, large orders or deals between cities, and it leaves sellers exposed to returns and cancelled orders.

**Escrow** solves the standoff by putting a neutral party in the middle.

## How escrow works, step by step

1. **The deal is agreed.** The seller sends the buyer a payment request that describes what will be delivered, the price and the delivery terms.
2. **Both sides are verified.** The escrow service checks the identity of the buyer and the seller, which discourages fraud before any money moves.
3. **The buyer pays into escrow.** The money does not go to the seller. It is held safely by the escrow service through a licensed payment gateway.
4. **The seller delivers.** Because the money is already secured, the seller can start work or ship the product with confidence, and uploads proof of delivery.
5. **The buyer confirms.** The buyer checks that what arrived matches what was agreed.
6. **The money is released.** Only after confirmation does the escrow service pay the seller, to their bank account or mobile wallet.

## What happens when something goes wrong

Sometimes the product does not match, or the work is incomplete. With escrow, the buyer can raise a dispute **before** the money is released. The funds stay locked while a neutral mediator reviews evidence from both sides. The outcome might be a replacement, a partial refund or a full refund, depending on what was agreed. Neither side can simply walk away with the money.

## Why it helps both sides

**For buyers**
- No need to trust a stranger with an advance payment
- Money is returned if the seller does not deliver as agreed
- A clear record of what was promised

**For sellers**
- Proof that the buyer has actually paid before you spend time or stock
- Fewer fake orders and last-minute cancellations
- Customers who were hesitant to pay in advance are more willing to buy

## When escrow makes the most sense

Escrow is most useful when the amount is significant, the buyer and seller have not dealt with each other before, or delivery takes time. Freelance and agency projects, wholesale and B2B orders, high-value second-hand goods and pre-orders are good examples.
