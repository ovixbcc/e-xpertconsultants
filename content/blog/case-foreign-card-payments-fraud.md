Bangladesh has a large and growing community of **freelancers, exporters and tech service providers** who sell to customers abroad. To get paid smoothly, many of them need to accept **international card payments** through a local payment gateway. For a gateway, these merchants are an important growth market.

They also bring a serious risk.

## The problem: fake merchants and chargebacks

Fraudsters open merchant accounts using **fake documents**, such as forged trade licenses, borrowed identities and websites that look real but sell nothing. They then run stolen international cards through those accounts and withdraw the money before anyone notices.

When the real cardholders dispute the charges, the result is a wave of **chargebacks**. The gateway, and through it the acquiring bank, has to absorb the losses. If chargeback rates stay high, card networks can impose penalties or restrict international acceptance for the gateway altogether. That puts **every honest merchant** on the platform at risk, not just the fraudsters.

## Why simple rules do not work

Blocking all foreign cards or all new merchants would stop fraud but also stop growth and hurt genuine freelancers and exporters. The answer is to know your merchants better and watch transactions more closely, without making onboarding painful.

## A layered approach

**1. Stronger merchant onboarding (KYB).**
- Verify NID and trade license details against official sources where possible
- Check bank account ownership matches the business
- Review the website or portfolio: real products, clear refund policy, working contact details
- Ask freelancers for proof of real work, such as marketplace profiles or client contracts

**2. Risk-based limits for new merchants.** Start new accounts with lower transaction and payout limits, and raise them as the merchant builds a clean history.

**3. Delayed or rolling payouts.** Hold a share of international card funds for a period before settlement, so money is still available if chargebacks arrive.

**4. Real-time transaction monitoring.** Flag unusual patterns: many cards from different countries in a short time, mismatched IP and card country, repeated small test payments, or sudden volume spikes.

**5. 3-D Secure and card network tools.** Require cardholder authentication where possible and use the fraud tools that card networks provide.

**6. Fast response to disputes.** Track chargeback ratios per merchant every day and freeze payouts quickly when they rise.

## Balancing growth and safety

The goal is not to say no. It is to make it **easy for genuine merchants** to get approved and paid, and **expensive and slow for fraudsters**. Good onboarding, sensible limits and monitoring do both.

Escrow-style controls help too. Holding funds until delivery is confirmed, the model behind **[TrustBee](https://trustbee.com.bd/)**, reduces the chance of paying out money that later has to be returned.
