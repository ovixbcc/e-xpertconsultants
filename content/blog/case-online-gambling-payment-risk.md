Online gambling has grown sharply in Bangladesh over the past year. It is illegal, it targets young people, and it is quietly draining money out of families and out of the country. For anyone working in payments, it is also a direct risk: gambling operators depend on local payment channels to collect money.

## How the problem shows up

From the outside, the pattern is clear:

- **Aggressive advertising** on Facebook, TikTok and YouTube, often aimed at students and young people, promising easy money.
- **Collection through local accounts.** Deposits are taken through bank accounts and mobile wallets opened with **fake or borrowed documents**, including fake trade licenses.
- **Local middlemen** manage these accounts in exchange for a percentage.
- **Money leaves the country**, often disguised as payments for goods or services that never existed.

The human cost is high. Young people lose savings, borrow to chase losses and fall into debt and despair. Many are scammed outright, because the "winnings" never arrive.

## Why payment systems are the key control point

Gambling operators can change websites and ad accounts every day. What they cannot easily replace is a reliable way to **collect money locally**. That makes banks, mobile financial services, payment gateways and platforms the most effective place to cut them off.

## What payment providers can do

**1. Stronger account and merchant verification.** Check that trade licenses, NIDs and business activity are genuine and match. Accounts opened with borrowed or fake documents are the backbone of these schemes.

**2. Watch for collection patterns.** Accounts that suddenly receive many small deposits from many different individuals, especially young users and late at night, followed by quick large transfers out, deserve review.

**3. Link related accounts.** The same device, phone number, address or agent behind many accounts is a strong signal of a network, not a business.

**4. Scrutinise "trade" payments abroad.** Outward payments for goods or services with no matching delivery, unusual counterparties or round-number patterns should be checked against supporting documents.

**5. Report and share intelligence.** Suspicious activity should be reported to the Bangladesh Financial Intelligence Unit, and providers benefit from sharing typologies with each other.

## What platforms and families can do

- **Social media platforms** should act quickly on gambling ads and the pages behind them, and users can report them.
- **Parents and teachers** can talk openly with young people about how these "earn fast" offers work and why the house always wins.
- **Anyone who has been scammed** should report it and seek help rather than trying to win the money back.

## The role of good systems

Most of these controls are not new. They are standard know-your-customer, transaction monitoring and reporting practices, applied consistently and with the right data. Automation makes them practical at scale: rules that flag patterns in real time, dashboards for risk teams and clean audit trails for regulators.
